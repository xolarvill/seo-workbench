# Turn evidence into useful prose

Use when a brief, project rule or verification note limits what an author can claim. The [reader method](../SKILL.md) decides how the supported answer is expressed.

## Select the claim before drafting

Identify the reader's question and the facts that support an answer. For an unsupported claim, remove it, narrow it to the supported scope, or obtain the missing essential input. Do not draft the broad claim and then attach a general disclaimer to compensate.

Keep the evidence record with the existing task or editorial artifact: source, check date, uncertainty, failed fetch, missing interview and unperformed test. These records help the agent choose claims. They are not instructions to repeat each gap in the public article.

## Decide what readers need

| Input | Editorial handling | Reader-facing handling |
|---|---|---|
| No controlled productivity study for a cable guide | Do not claim a measured productivity gain | Give the supported routing steps; omit an unsolicited productivity disclaimer |
| No hands-on test, but manufacturer instructions are available | Attribute the instructions accurately; make no testing claim | Explain the procedure from those instructions; no generic “we have not tested this” preface |
| A compatibility condition affects the connection | Keep the relevant specification and source | Give the condition and check next to the action, such as checking video-output support |
| A load rating is unknown and needed for a product recommendation | Obtain the essential specification or withhold that recommendation | Identify the needed model/rating if the reader's decision cannot proceed; do not invent a limit or use “no guarantee” as a substitute |
| A figure is illustrative | Keep source and illustration status | Use a clear caption once; repeat only for a distinct interpretation problem |
| An outcome report lacks a comparison needed for attribution | Preserve windows, coverage and the causal gap | Describe observed change and the attribution limit when that is the reader's question; do not present an association as an established effect |
| A recommendation has not yet been tested | Keep the recommendation grounded in observations; make no measured-uplift claim | Give the change and useful follow-up metric; discuss effect uncertainty when the reader asks for a forecast or evidence verdict, rather than adding a routine no-guarantee closer |
| Sponsorship, a safety restriction or another material disclosure applies | Preserve the established facts and applicable requirement | State the needed disclosure or restriction where the reader uses it |

“Reader-facing” includes reports whose purpose is to evaluate evidence. Their readers may need methods and uncertainty. Decide from that task, rather than hiding limitations or inserting them into every article.

## Draft, then check the actual passage

Write the answer or action first. Add a condition only when omitting it would change a decision, make an instruction unsafe, misattribute a source or misrepresent an example/result. State the concrete condition and what to check. A sentence denying a promise or motive that nobody introduced normally adds no such information.

Read the whole passage. Remove repeated declarations of what the article does not prove. Keep accurate source attribution, real distinctions and necessary warnings, including useful negative instructions. Do not replace the removed defense with a new reassurance or an invented comparison.

## Keep delivery aligned with the request

When asked for article body only, deliver that body. Keep task status, evidence pointers, review findings and pending inputs in the existing task artifact. Provide an editorial report separately only when it is part of the requested handoff. A small edit does not need a new evidence file or appendix.
