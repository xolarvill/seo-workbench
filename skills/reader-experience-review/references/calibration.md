# Context-sensitive calibration

These examples illustrate judgment, not a replacement blacklist.

## Internal instruction versus useful product advice

Buying guide: “Do not copy the Pro model's charging capabilities into the Basic model.”

The buyer is not copying a specification into a draft. Replace with: “Compare the charging specifications for the exact model and regional version you plan to buy.” Keep any established model differences.

By contrast, “A USB-C charging socket does not necessarily carry video” addresses a plausible buying mistake. Keep it when it answers the article's question.

## Editorial ownership versus a useful link

“The cable guide owns the movement task” describes internal content allocation. “Use the cable guide to check the route as the desktop rises” explains why a reader would follow the link. Preserve the link and its actual scope.

## Defensible caution versus an unrelated rebuttal

“These are planning values, not measurements of a supplied desk” can keep a worked example from being mistaken for tested product data. A caption warning that a photograph does not establish compatibility can also be useful. Neither needs removal just because it is cautious.

“Camera exposure cannot establish medical benefits” is an unexplained detour in instructions to sit down and look for a reflection, unless photography or a health claim is actually being discussed. Remove the detour, not the practical check.

“Conceptual layout; not an installation drawing” is a useful image label. Retain its meaning even if a long repeated caption is shortened.

## A real gap versus a stylistic preference

An ultrawide guide that suddenly calculates the width of two smaller monitors needs a bridge explaining the comparison. Preserve the source, numbers and base-support caveat.

“If the lamp base blocks your mouse area. A monitor light may help” contains a broken conditional sentence. Join the clauses.

A single “Choose for long-term use” followed by a relevant keep-your-existing-lamp option may be acceptable, even if another editor could trim it. Repetition across a batch warrants inspection, not automatic deletion.

## Negative instructions belong in claim selection

If the brief says “do not imply hands-on testing,” label real sources and hypothetical examples accurately. Do not add an unsolicited “We have not tested this setup” solely to demonstrate compliance. Add clarification only when the existing passage would otherwise imply a test.

Technical phrases such as “heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors” retain their meaning even if a general style list dislikes “robust.” Preserve correct terminology and explain it for the audience if needed.
