# Technique 01: Information Gain Writing

## Purpose

Find useful information, explanation or synthesis beyond repetitive coverage. Information gain is an editorial aim: a patent or metric name does not prove that a particular draft receives a score or will rank higher. Judge additions by what they help the reader understand, do or decide.

## 1. Compare relevant existing coverage

Reuse the approved brief and current research when sufficient. For a new competitive topic, inspect relevant results and create a coverage map when helpful: sources, questions answered, evidence, conditions, disagreements and omissions. Record source URLs, dates and access limits.

Distinguish a genuine unanswered reader need from a topic a competitor omitted because it is irrelevant. Do not require every article or paragraph to reveal something exclusive.

## 2. Identify available value

Possible additions include:

- Original first-party data with a defined population, period and method.
- Genuine tests or case studies with conditions, results and limits.
- Attributed interviews or author experience relevant to the question.
- A verified process, prerequisites or documented failure modes.
- Analysis that compares sources, explains a tradeoff or resolves a confusing difference.
- A useful application of authoritative instructions to the intended reader's situation.

Select additions based on evidence and usefulness, without a required count or percentage. If original research is absent, accurately attributed synthesis can fulfill the brief. Do not imply that synthesis is testing or claim a unique discovery without checking it.

## 3. Plan and draft

Put the answer and the most useful distinctions where readers need them. Introduce prerequisites before dependent actions. Necessary basics and common facts deserve space when they support understanding; do not bury them to satisfy a novelty quota.

Map substantial additions to sections and supporting sources. Replace vague claims with precise details when the evidence supplies them. A hypothetical can clarify a mechanism if identified as illustrative; do not invent results, clients, quotations or exact values to imply experience.

Contrarian analysis is useful only when an actual claim, evidence and reader consequence support it. Do not manufacture “most guides get this wrong” framing or presume that losing rankings proves a lack of originality.

## 4. Review the contribution

Ask what the reader can now understand or do more reliably. Does the new section add supported information or merely restate the same material in another format? A table can still improve comparison and usability even when it adds no new facts; describe that benefit accurately.

Verify first-party claims and attribution. If the brief promises a test, interview or proprietary analysis that is missing, identify the exact gap, narrow the promise when authorized or obtain the required evidence. Do not block unrelated drafting for optional anecdotes.

## Deliverable

Record the useful contribution, its source and relevant limits in the existing task artifact. Where no new finding is available, state the actual value: a concise answer, verified steps or a clearer synthesis. Do not claim exclusive information or SEO effect without evidence.

## Tools Used

- keyword search and competitor data: relevant existing coverage.
- SERP feature detection: questions and answer formats to investigate.
- opportunity detection and page-level SEO data: observed performance and candidate gaps for updates.
