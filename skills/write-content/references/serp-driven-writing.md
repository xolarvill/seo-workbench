# Technique 05: SERP-Informed Writing

## Purpose

Use current search results to understand intent, reader expectations, competing coverage and useful opportunities. Ranking pages are observations, not proof that their length, pronouns, links or headings caused rankings. The approved brief, factual evidence and reader task govern the draft.

## 1. Decide whether research is needed

Reuse sufficiently current, relevant research. A new competitive article, changed intent or a material gap may warrant fresh SERP analysis. An approved brief or small wording review does not automatically require a new top-results study.

For a substantive search, record query, market, language, device or search context when available, collection date and result URLs. Note access limits and avoid treating an inaccessible page as absent coverage.

## 2. Map intent and coverage

Inspect a bounded sample of relevant results, expanding only when needed to resolve uncertainty. Record:

- Dominant intent and content format: instructions, explanation, comparison, product or local service.
- Questions answered, prerequisites, evaluation criteria and technical depth.
- Relevant snippets, People Also Ask questions, tables, videos or other features.
- Evidence quality, publication or update dates and unsupported competitor claims.
- Agreements, disagreements, omissions and audience-specific use cases.

Use a coverage matrix when several sources or topics need comparison. Separate the observed pattern from the proposed editorial decision and its reason.

## 3. Build or review the outline

Choose sections that fulfill the reader's task. Competitor headings and word counts are context, not a structure to copy or a length target. A narrow question may need only a short answer; a comparison needs enough space for criteria, evidence and tradeoffs.

Place the useful answer early. Retain necessary basics and safety steps even when common across sources. Add verified analysis or missing applications when useful; do not force an original section or an opposing viewpoint into every article.

Map natural topic terminology to titles and headings without density targets, exact-match quotas or prescribed H2 counts. Avoid creating a page that duplicates an existing site's topic owner without an explicit differentiation or consolidation decision.

## 4. Format relevant answers

If a question needs steps, use steps. Use a table for comparable attributes and prose for conditions or explanations. Existing search features can inform the format but do not compel it.

Answer relevant PAA questions directly when they fit the brief. Do not add a fixed number of questions, repeat exact search wording unnaturally or expand every answer to the same word count. Structured data needs a separate implementation and eligibility check.

## 5. Evaluate trust and differentiation

Check the actual basis for expertise and experience. Genuine testing, specific case studies and original images can help when available and relevant. Pronouns alone are not evidence. An accurately attributed manufacturer procedure can be useful without an interview or personal anecdote.

Explain limitations and attribute material claims. Do not infer how a ranking algorithm evaluates a particular draft from a patent, leak, anecdotal experiment or correlation. Source interpretations belong in research notes with their limits, not as guaranteed writing recipes.

## 6. Freshness and outcome review

Set verification and update needs according to how quickly the topic changes. Update the substance and verification date when appropriate; do not alter dates to imply new research that did not occur.

After publication within the authorized workflow, review the actual site's comparable search evidence. A rise or fall in rankings does not by itself establish which editorial choice caused it.

## Deliverable

For a new article, retain the intent summary, source/coverage map, useful gaps and outline decisions. For an approved brief, record only material conflicts or missing evidence; continue writing when those do not block the task.
