# Technique 02: Plain, Specific Writing Review

## Purpose and scope

Review wording for clarity, accuracy and usefulness. This is an editorial method, not an AI authorship detector. Word choice, sentence length and polished grammar cannot establish who wrote a passage or predict its search performance.

Use this method for a full draft or a small passage. Scale the review to the reader's task. Short maintenance instructions can already be complete; zero edits is a valid result.

## Pass 1: Find a reader problem

Read the brief, passage and relevant evidence together. Check:

- **Inflated language:** Replace jargon, empty praise and abstract verbs when a familiar, precise word works better. Keep technical terms when they help the intended reader.
- **Empty transitions:** Remove phrases such as “it's worth noting” when the next sentence can stand on its own. Keep transitions that explain a real relationship.
- **Vague claims:** Identify missing subjects, conditions, dates or quantities. Add detail only from available evidence; otherwise narrow the claim or record the missing input.
- **Unsupported significance:** Remove superlatives and claims of importance that the evidence does not support.
- **Unclear references:** Resolve ambiguous pronouns, inconsistent terminology and synonyms that make one object look like several.
- **Repetition:** Cut repeated explanations, unnecessary section summaries and padded introductions. Keep repetition that helps readers follow safety instructions or identify the same component.
- **Structure:** Put the answer, action or decision where readers need it. Review lists, examples and headings for actual usefulness rather than changing their number or shape.
- **Rhetorical framing:** State the actual point directly. Remove an invented opponent, unnecessary “not X but Y” framing, rhetorical questions or dramatic fragments when they obscure it.

For each proposed edit, identify the passage, the reader problem and the reason the change helps. Do not flag words or patterns solely because they appear on an inherited banned list.

## Pass 2: Check the revision

1. Compare the revision with the original and the source evidence.
2. Preserve instructions, technical meaning, qualifications, attribution and author intent.
3. Check that the edit has not introduced a new claim, ambiguity or needless explanation.
4. Read it aloud when useful to catch awkward wording; the appropriate register depends on the format and audience.
5. Stop when the passage works. Do not rewrite solely to create more edits or a different statistical signature.

## Specificity check

Ask whether the passage tells this reader what they need to know. Exact steps, eligibility conditions, product limits and accurately attributed facts can supply specificity without an anecdote. A common fact or standard procedure remains useful when it answers the task; it need not be unique to this author or page.

If the brief promises a comparison, original analysis or personal testing, verify that the promised evidence exists. If it does not, narrow the promise or flag the precise missing evidence. Do not invent names, quotations, test results, dates or experience.

## Voice and rhythm

Use an available, genuine voice sample to guide register and terminology. Preserve clear prose when no sample is available. Do not add quirks, deliberate awkwardness, tangents, jokes, first-person claims or sentence fragments to simulate personality. Do not measure sentence-length variation as a gate or lengthen a clear instruction for rhythm. Match sentence boundaries to the ideas and actions.

## Deliverable

Return the necessary edits with reasons and any unresolved factual inputs. If no change helps, retain the passage and say so. A checklist result describes observed editorial issues; it is not an AI probability, publication approval or ranking forecast.
