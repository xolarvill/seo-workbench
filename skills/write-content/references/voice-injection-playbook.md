# Technique 03: Voice and Register

## Purpose

Voice samples are inputs to [the reader method](../../reader-experience-review/SKILL.md), which alone decides expression. Use this reference to extract truthful register and terminology evidence, not to run another style pass.

Preserve a recognizable author or brand voice while helping the intended reader. Use documented examples rather than inventing a persona, personal history or stylistic quirks. Personality is optional; clear, plain instructions may be the correct voice.

## 1. Collect relevant samples

Reuse existing brand guidance and genuine writing samples before requesting more. Prefer samples from the same author, audience and channel: a maintenance guide, product page and newsletter may require different registers. Collect enough material to identify a pattern, without a fixed sample count or word minimum.

Record the author or source, content format and approximate currency of each sample. Negative examples can help: terms the brand avoids, misleading promises or a tone the author dislikes. If samples are unavailable, use the brief's audience and purpose to choose a plain register, state the assumption when material, and proceed without claiming an exact voice match.

## 2. Build a compact voice profile

Capture only observed and useful characteristics:

- Reader, task and expected knowledge.
- Register and degree of formality by channel.
- Preferred terminology and naming conventions.
- How the author explains technical ideas and qualifies claims.
- Use of humor, contractions or direct address when present in real samples.
- A few short, attributed excerpts demonstrating these choices.

Do not infer credentials, opinions or experiences from a requested tone. Keep the profile close to the work and refresh it when the samples no longer represent the author.

## 3. Apply voice to the task

Write the explanation, answer or action first. Use concrete details when they help the reader understand or decide, and only when evidence supports them.

- **Instructions and definitions:** Answer first; use explicit steps and stable component names.
- **Comparisons and data pages:** Explain criteria, evidence, limits and tradeoffs.
- **Opinion pieces:** Attribute the actual author's position and support its reasoning. Disagreement requires an identifiable claim and evidence; do not invent what “everyone” or “most guides” supposedly says.
- **Brand and service pages:** Relate relevant capabilities to reader needs. Use a story or scenario only when it explains something the reader needs.
- **Newsletters and personal essays:** More conversational phrasing may fit documented voice; it is not a requirement for other formats.

Choose pronouns by meaning. Chinese instructions can address the reader without an explicit “you.” Use “I” or “we” for genuine, attributable involvement; do not introduce them as trust or search-ranking signals.

A hypothetical example may illustrate a mechanism if clearly identified as hypothetical. It must not masquerade as testing, a customer story or expert testimony. Do not add exact numbers merely to make an example sound real.

## 4. Review voice without manufacturing it

Compare the draft with the relevant samples for terminology, register and explanatory habits. Read aloud if it helps find awkward wording. Keep precise technical language when it serves the audience.

Sentence lengths and paragraph shapes should follow meaning. Do not enforce variation, add fragments, introduce deliberate mistakes or insert casual asides to imitate human authorship. A consistent register and several similar-length instructions can be appropriate.

Check that voice changes have not altered facts or obscured the answer. Keep some sentences entirely plain; stop when the passage is clear and appropriate.

## Deliverable

For substantive work, keep the samples and any useful voice profile in the existing task artifact. Return only the requested copy unless a voice analysis is part of the handoff. A local edit needs no new profile, explanation or extra deliverable. There is no required share of personality, anecdotes or proprietary details.
